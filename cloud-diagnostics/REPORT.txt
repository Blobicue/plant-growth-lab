4. CONTRACT AUDIT AND CORRECTION PROPOSAL
No fix is justified as an existing-contract bug. Seeds are paid equally, genes
are copied, resources are conserved, original fingerprints reproduce, and local
construction/maintenance behave as written. The32-node gate blocks gen3 because
juveniles never build the required structure; lowering it would hide the problem.
The14h proposal expiry and passive transport are unchanged. Earlier diagnostics28/29
found retention alone did not restore paid growth; none of that negative evidence
has been overwritten or used to justify another retention change.

The narrowest justified next DESIGN QUESTION is whether a juvenile may direct
its already-owned mobile N toward an active root-construction sink through its
existing connected transport graph. At the first refusal there is enough N in
the plant as a whole but not at the paying front. This is a proposed single-policy
allocation experiment, NOT an approved correction and NOT a promise of success.
It would trade root acquisition against other tissue's maintenance/leaf/bud N;
any active transport work/capacity must be specified and paid rather than silently
made free. W27 deliberately excludes experimental growth allocators, so this
requires an explicit revised model contract before implementation. No such policy
has been implemented here.

Longer-term N/W recycling is a separate ecological design question. Returning
old/dead pools could improve availability, but would add a release/transport law
and may alter occupancy and competition; existing carbon-only decomposition is
not a complete nutrient-cycle model. Do not combine this with root allocation or
change uptake constants, seed budgets,32-node maturity or genes to force success.
The resource-exclusion controls are explanations, not proposed production rules.
